What the growth curve means

Impressions are up 806% quarter-over-quarter, with a clear liftoff in early July. Google is now indexing and testing your pages against far more searches than it was two months ago. That is the leading indicator of momentum — clicks and calls follow once your positions climb from page two onto page one.

Brand searches — already working
  • 17 of your 69 clicks came from just 204 impressions where someone searched "TopFlight" or "Top Flight Builders."
  • Average position 1.3, with a 25–30% click-through rate.
  • When people already know your name, they find you and click instantly. That part is solved.
Non-brand searches — the opportunity
  • The other 4,059 impressions (kitchen, bath, remodeling, city terms) produced just 1 click.
  • Those listings sit on page 2–3 — positions 16 to 31 — where almost no one clicks.
  • You are being seen by new customers, not yet chosen by them.

On Google

Strong on restoration. Quiet on
remodeling. Absent from the map.

Across live searches and your Search Console data, one clear pattern emerges. You win the specific, high-intent restoration searches, you're buried on the broad remodeling terms, and you're not yet in the local map results at all.

1

Restoration is your win

You're the #1 organic result for "storm and fallen tree damage home repair contractor Marietta GA," above Relief Remediation, Capital, and Integrity. Your data agrees: "home restoration near me" sits at position 7 and "tenant improvement Canton" at position 4.

2

Remodeling is the gap

Kitchen, bath, and whole-home city terms rank on page 2–3 (positions 16–31). Directories like Houzz, Thumbtack, and Yelp, plus established design-build brands, own page one for these searches today.

3

The Map Pack is missing

On both remodeling and restoration searches, Google's local 3-pack shows Integrity, Big House, and AEM. You are not in it. Each of them carries 250+ Google reviews; you're at roughly 50 — the clearest thing separating you.

Why the gap — and why it's fixable

AI engines lean on the same signals as the Map Pack: third-party review counts, directory presence (Houzz, Yelp, BBB), and consistent mentions across the web. Next Steps

Four moves, in priority order.

Each one compounds: reviews lift both your map ranking and your AI citations, restoration content feeds the searches you already win, and the remodeling pages are a short climb from page one.

01

Build a reviews engine

Close the 250-vs-50 gap with an ask-for-review step after every job. Biggest single lever for both the map and AI.

02

Own restoration

Double down where you already win — city storm, tree, and water-damage pages with insurance-claim stories.

03

Rescue remodeling pages

Push the kitchen, bath, and city pages from position 16–23 onto page one with stronger content and local signals.

04

Feed the AI engines

Grow /ai-info, keep name-address-phone consistent across Houzz, Yelp, and BBB, add FAQ and structured data.
